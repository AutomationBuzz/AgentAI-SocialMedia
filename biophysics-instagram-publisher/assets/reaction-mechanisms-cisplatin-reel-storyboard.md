## 000-015 seconds
Four patterns. One decision tree.

Substitution replaces a group. Elimination makes a double bond.

Visual: overview, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Four fundamental patterns help organize reactions at carbon. Substitution swaps one group for another. Elimination removes groups to form a carbon carbon double bond. Each can follow two common pathways.

## 015-030 seconds
Meet the reaction cast.

A substrate carries the leaving group. A nucleophile donates an electron pair.

Visual: parts, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Meet the cast. The substrate is the molecule being changed. A leaving group departs with the bonding electron pair. A nucleophile donates an electron pair to an electron-poor carbon. A base can remove a nearby hydrogen.

## 030-045 seconds
SN2: one step. Two bonds change.

Nucleophile attacks as the leaving group departs. Crowding slows this route.

Visual: sn2, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: In S N two, the nucleophile attacks the carbon as the leaving group departs, in one concerted step. A crowded carbon makes approach harder. A good nucleophile and leaving group can help, but solvent and substrate also matter.

## 045-060 seconds
SN1: leave, then attack.

Leaving-group departure forms a carbocation; a nucleophile traps it in a later step.

Visual: sn1, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: In S N one, the leaving group departs first. That creates a carbocation, a positively charged carbon intermediate. A nucleophile attacks afterward. Carbocation stability and solvent influence this pathway; it is not simply a stronger-nucleophile contest.

## 060-075 seconds
Substitution or elimination?

A carbocation can lead to substitution or elimination; substrate and conditions matter.

Visual: competition, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Once a carbocation forms, substitution and elimination can compete. A nucleophile can bond to the positively charged carbon. Or a base can remove a neighboring hydrogen and form a double bond. Structure, solvent, temperature, and reagent shape the outcome.

## 075-090 seconds
E2: remove H. Lose the leaving group.

A base removes a beta hydrogen as the leaving group departs: one concerted step.

Visual: e2, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: In E two, a base removes a beta hydrogen while the leaving group departs from the adjacent carbon. The double bond forms in that same concerted step. Geometry matters: the hydrogen and leaving group must align appropriately.

## 090-105 seconds
E1: a shared carbocation route.

Leaving-group departure comes first; then a base removes beta H to form an alkene.

Visual: e1, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: In E one, the leaving group departs first to form a carbocation. Then a base removes a beta hydrogen, and a double bond forms. Because S N one and E one share an intermediate, their products may compete.

## 105-120 seconds
Read the conditions, not slogans.

Substrate, nucleophile or base, solvent, and heat shift competition; shortcuts have exceptions.

Visual: matrix, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Use the four labels as a starting map, not automatic answers. Carbon substitution, nucleophile or base strength, leaving-group ability, solvent, and heat all matter. Real reactions can be mixed or depart from simple textbook trends.

## 120-135 seconds
A clinical bridge. A different chemistry.

Cisplatin is FDA-approved in defined cancer settings, but it is not an SN1, SN2, E1, or E2 reaction.

Visual: bridge, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Now a real-world connection, with an important boundary. Cisplatin is an FDA-approved platinum chemotherapy used in certain bladder, ovarian, and testicular cancer settings. Its chemistry is not S N one, S N two, E one, or E two.

## 135-150 seconds
Platinum trades ligands in water.

Aquation and platinum ligand substitution enable DNA adducts and crosslinks that disrupt DNA function.

Visual: aquation, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Cisplatin undergoes platinum ligand substitution, including aquation in biological conditions. Platinum can form DNA adducts and crosslinks, disrupting DNA replication and repair. That is coordination chemistry at platinum, not substitution at an organic carbon or an elimination pathway.

## 150-165 seconds

Cisplatin is clinician-administered; kidney, hearing, nerve, and severe nausea risks require protocol-based care.

Visual: clinical, original vector-like schematic motion.

Narration: Cisplatin can cause serious kidney injury, hearing damage, nausea and vomiting, and peripheral neuropathy. The FDA-submitted label says it should be given under qualified physician supervision. Treatment and risks depend on the indication and individual protocol; this is not personal medical advice.
