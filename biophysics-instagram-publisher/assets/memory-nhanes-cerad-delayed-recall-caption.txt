What does a low score on one memory task actually tell us? In CDC NHANES 2011-2012, 1,451 completed CERAD delayed word-recall tests (ages 60-80) had scores from 0 to 10. Using the NHANES MEC examination weights, 9.59% fell in the 0-2 score band; 175 people were in that band (12.06% of this tested sample, unweighted).

Important: 0-2 is a visualization band chosen for this graphic, NOT a clinical cutoff or diagnosis. This one older survey cycle is not current prevalence; a single word-recall task does not define a person's memory or health. The bars show weighted descriptive shares; no confidence interval is displayed.

The sleep research connection is separate: Chang et al. studied mice, not people. Animal findings help generate questions but do not prove the same mechanism in humans.
