Predict. Measure. Refine.

Biophysics + AI works best as a conversation between physical models, machine learning and experiments.

Physical models describe interactions and geometry. Machine learning can learn patterns from data while incorporating physical and biological constraints. Experiments provide measurements against which predictions can be tested.

One example: AlphaFold predicts protein structures and estimates confidence. Jumper et al. (Nature, 2021) evaluated its predictions against experimentally determined structures, including the blind CASP14 assessment. A predicted structure is not itself a measurement, and does not by itself establish protein dynamics or biological function.

Good validation asks: How uncertain is the prediction? How does it compare with independent measurements and their experimental error? Does it still work out-of-distribution, on new proteins or conditions beyond the training data? Confidence and physical constraints are not guarantees of correctness.

Use models to sharpen hypotheses, experiments to challenge them, and the results to refine the next model. AI complements experiments; it does not replace them.
